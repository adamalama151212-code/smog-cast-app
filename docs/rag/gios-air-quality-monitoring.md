# GIOŚ and air-quality measurements in Poland – unofficial English translation

Source: Chief Inspectorate of Environmental Protection (Główny Inspektorat Ochrony Środowiska, GIOŚ), pages "Kim
jesteśmy" (https://powietrze.gios.gov.pl/pjp/content/about_us) and "Informacje ogólne"
(https://powietrze.gios.gov.pl/pjp/content/measuring_air_assessment_measurings), read on 9 October 2026. This is an
unofficial translation of the parts about who measures the air and how; the Polish pages are the official text.

## Who GIOŚ is

"The Inspection for Environmental Protection is established to check compliance with environmental protection law and
to study and assess the state of the environment. The Inspection consists of the Chief Inspectorate of Environmental
Protection (GIOŚ) and 16 voivodeship inspectorates of environmental protection."

"Air-quality monitoring within the State Environmental Monitoring (PMŚ) is coordinated and carried out by the Chief
Inspector of Environmental Protection, in accordance with the Act of 10 July 1991 on the Inspection for Environmental
Protection." Its aim is to obtain information and data on the levels of substances in the ambient air.

## How the air is measured

- Under the Environmental Protection Law and its regulations, GIOŚ measures the concentrations of sulphur dioxide,
  nitrogen dioxide and nitrogen oxides, benzene, carbon monoxide, ozone, **particulate matter PM10 and PM2.5**, and
  lead, arsenic, cadmium, nickel and benzo(a)pyrene in PM10.
- The monitoring system is based on networks of measuring stations placed where they are needed in each voivodeship,
  mostly in cities, where analyses show high concentrations of pollutants.
- There are **288 stations** in Poland, of which **219** are automatic or automatic-manual; their **hourly** results
  are published as they come on the "Jakość Powietrza" (Air Quality) portal and in the GIOŚ mobile apps. In total there
  are about 1,800 measuring positions: about 1,050 automatic and about 750 manual.
- **Manual** measurements (filters analysed in a laboratory) are published only after about **1–1.5 months**, because
  of the laboratory procedures.

## What this means for smogcast

- smogcast uses the **automatic hourly** PM10 and PM2.5 results of the stations in the 10 largest Polish cities:
  the live results for today's forecast and the yearly archive of GIOŚ (2021–2025) for learning and testing.
- Manual positions have no live hourly data, so they cannot be used for tomorrow's forecast.
- Live hourly results are preliminary; GIOŚ verifies the data later and publishes the verified results in its yearly
  archive. smogcast therefore marks measured values from the live feed as not yet verified.
