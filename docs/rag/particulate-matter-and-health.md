# Particulate matter (PM10 and PM2.5) and health – based on the European Environment Agency

Source: European Environment Agency (EEA), "How air pollution affects our health"
(https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/topics/in-depth/air-pollution/how-it-affects-our-health) and "Air quality status report
2025 – Particulate matter" (https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/air-quality-status-report-2025/particulate-matter),
read on 9 October 2026. The quoted sentences are the EEA's own words; the rest is a short guide to them.

## What particulate matter is

Particulate matter (PM) is dust made of tiny particles in the air. The two sizes measured in Poland are:
- **PM10** – "particulate matter with a diameter of 10 µm or less" (EEA);
- **PM2.5** – fine particulate matter, 2.5 µm or less: a part of PM10 small enough to get deep into the lungs.

In smogcast, a **smog day** means a day when the daily mean of PM10 or PM2.5 at the worst station of a city is above
the daily limit.

## Where it comes from

- "PM10 is emitted mainly by the combustion of solid fuels for domestic heating, although industrial activities,
  agriculture and road transport are also important sources." (EEA)
- "In many central and eastern European countries, solid fuels such as coal and wood are used for heating households
  and in some industrial facilities and power plants." (EEA) This is why smog in Polish cities comes mostly in the
  heating season.
- Natural sources add to it: "sea salt, Saharan dust or volcanoes", and some particles form in the air itself "as a
  combination of different gases (for instance, ammonia and nitrogen dioxide)" (EEA).
- "Residential, commercial and institutional energy consumption was the principal source of particulate matter in
  2023. The manufacturing and extractive industry and transport were also significant sources, while agriculture was an
  important source of PM10." (EEA)

## How it affects health

- "Air pollution is the single largest environmental health risk in Europe and a major cause of premature death and
  disease." (EEA)
- "Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) is the air pollutant driving the most significant health problems and premature
  mortality." (EEA) The EEA estimates that in 2023 "approximately 182,000 premature deaths were attributable to PM2.5
  in the 27 EU Member States".
- "Both short- and long-term exposure to air pollution can lead to a wide range of diseases, including stroke, chronic
  obstructive pulmonary disease, trachea, bronchus and lung cancers, aggravated asthma and lower respiratory
  infections." Long exposure "can affect every organ in the body". (EEA)
- **Who is most at risk:** "Children and adolescents are particularly vulnerable because their bodies, organs and immune
  systems are still developing." The EEA also notes that "poorer people are more likely to live next to busy roads or
  industrial areas".

## Limits and guidelines for PM10 (EEA)

| | Daily | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| EU limit value | 50 µg/m³ | 40 µg/m³ |
| WHO guideline level | 45 µg/m³ | 15 µg/m³ |

"Concentrations above the EU daily limit value for PM10 are seen mainly in Italy and some eastern European countries."
(EEA)

## Is it getting better?

"Between 2005 and 2023, emissions of particulate matter, PM10 and PM2.5, fell by 36% and 38%, respectively." (EEA)
